# Extend the exact-pin, push-based propagation policy to Go module dependencies too

**Context:** Decision `016` established exact-pin, producer-triggered propagation for WASM builds consumed by web apps. It scoped itself to WASM specifically and explicitly left "cross-library shared-dependency conflicts" out of scope, on the grounds that no real conflict had occurred yet. One has now occurred, in a different shape than a *conflict*: `sff` released `v0.3.1` (and, separately, `stage` released a version incorporating it), but the Go module pins in `character/go.mod`, `stage/go.mod`, and `engine/go.mod` — plus the WASM download pins in `lifebar-viewer-web`, `lifebar-editor`, `stage-viewer-web`, and `stage-editor` — all went stale at once, unnoticed, because `016`'s producer-side propagation instruction only ever told a producer's `CLAUDE.md` to check its *WASM* consumers, never its Go-module ones. `sff` itself had no propagation section at all, despite being the most widely depended-on repo in the org (2 Go-module consumers, 4 WASM consumers).

**Decision:**
- The exact-pin, push-based propagation policy `016` established for WASM builds applies identically to Go module dependencies between the org's own repos (any `require github.com/openkakutou/X vY.Z.W` in a `go.mod`). Same mechanism, same trigger, same one substitution: `go get github.com/openkakutou/X@vY.Z.W && go mod tidy` in place of a WASM download.
- **Every producer repo's `CLAUDE.md` propagation section must enumerate *every* known consumer, by mechanism** — both its WASM-download consumers and its Go-module consumers — not just whichever mechanism happened to exist when the section was first written. `character` and `stage` each gained a Go-module consumer (`engine`) after their propagation sections were written and neither section was updated to mention it; `sff` never had a section at all despite being depended on both ways since before this decision. All three are corrected as part of this decision.
- **A dependency-only bump that produces no observable behavior change in the *bumping* repo's own tests does not, by itself, require that repo to cut a new release** — but if the underlying fix is worth surfacing to *that* repo's own downstream consumers (WASM or Go module), cutting one and continuing the propagation chain into its own section is the producer's call to make at bump time, not an automatic trigger.
- Still explicitly out of scope, per `016`: genuine cross-library version *conflicts* (two consumers of the same dependency needing incompatible versions) — no real instance of that has occurred yet either; this decision only closes the "propagation forgot a consumer mechanism" gap, not that one.

**Reason:** The actual failure mode is identical to the one `016` was written to fix — "a fix shipped and nobody told the consumer to pick it up" — just manifesting through a second mechanism (`go.mod`) that `016`'s own wording didn't cover and that several producers' `CLAUDE.md` sections consequently never mentioned. Generalizing the existing, already-proven mechanism (producer-triggered, no new infrastructure) is simpler than inventing a second, parallel policy for Go modules specifically.

**Rejected alternatives:**
- *A central cross-repo dependency registry tracking every pin*: rejected for the same reason `016` rejected it — still speculative machinery relative to the org's current size; each producer's own `CLAUDE.md` listing its own consumers by mechanism is sufficient and keeps the "read the one file every session already loads" property `016` specifically valued.
- *Automated CI that fails a producer's release if any consumer's pin is stale*: rejected — new always-running infrastructure for a need a human/agent-driven release step already handles once it actually checks the right list; revisit only if the manual propagation step itself proves unreliable at a larger consumer count.
